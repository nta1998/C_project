10 shots, 18 seconds total, 2.39:1, anamorphic. Hard cuts — fast-cut rhythm, every cut lands on an action. Slow motion ONLY inside SHOT 9. One continuous story: a man boards a moving train to reach a woman who is robbing it, both of them fight their way clear along the roofs — and she goes off a viaduct into the sea one second before he gets there.

SCENE ENGINE (production spine)
GOAL: reach HER before the train runs out of country. Her goal: finish the job and be gone before anyone lays a hand on her.
OBSTACLE: a train at full speed between them, and three guards on the roofs — one of them a good deal bigger than she is.
TACTIC: he does not stop it and does not shoot — he gets on it and comes up the roofs on foot. She does not run and does not hide; she takes what the bigger man gives her, waits for him to overcommit, and puts him down with his own weight — then keeps walking toward the edge she has already chosen.
REVERSAL: he does everything right and arrives exactly one second late — and the thing that takes her away this time is not a ship or a crowd, it is her own decision.
VALUE SHIFT: a rider chasing a train → a man who will get on a moving train for her → he is going to reach her → she was never going to be caught. Her verdict: someone behind me → him → and she goes anyway, and lets him see her go.

REFERENCE DEFINITIONS
@train: a late-1800s American West steam train at speed — a black locomotive with a diamond stack and cowcatcher throwing a bright white steam plume back over the whole train, a tender, then six weathered DARK RED wooden passenger carriages with cream window frames, iron roof walkways running the full length of every roof, and a narrow railed open platform with a grab rail at each carriage end; the last carriage ends in a rear platform with a curved iron rail. Dusty, sun-faded, hard-used. It runs on a single raised track across open high-desert scrub — dry grass, low sage, pale rock, distant mesas under a huge bleached sky. Geometry, materials and atmosphere only. Reference.
@He_train: young man — face, hair and wardrobe match the reference exactly in every frame: dark brown flat-brim hat worn level, long dark brown oiled canvas duster worn OPEN to mid-calf, charcoal waistcoat over a faded indigo henley with the sleeves pushed up, heavy dark trousers, worn brown riding boots, a plain leather gunbelt low on the hips with one holstered revolver on the RIGHT. Thick black wavy hair pressed flat under the hat; short full dark beard; sun-weathered dusty skin. Reference.
@She_train: young woman — face, hair and wardrobe match the reference exactly in every frame: dark charcoal flat-brim hat worn level and low, long faded-tan oiled canvas duster worn OPEN to mid-calf, dark slate work shirt buttoned to the throat with the sleeves rolled to the elbow, heavy dark trousers tucked into scuffed brown riding boots, a plain leather gunbelt low on the hips with one holstered revolver on the LEFT. Long dark-brown hair in one thick braid over her right shoulder; sun-weathered skin, wind burn on the cheekbones, trail dust. Reference.
@boards: the three-panel hand-drawn storyboard sheet. It governs THREE things and nothing else — the train's screen direction, the roof map, and the viaduct geometry. Its pencil line style, its white paper and its framing never appear in the film. Reference.

TECHNICAL BLOCK
Cinematic photoreal, ultra filmic classic western. 21:9 — 35mm anamorphic film character: horizontal squeeze and compression, oval elliptical bokeh with stretched highlights, curved barrel edge distortion, subtle chromatic aberration toward the edges — all subtle and moderate; NO lens flares, NO streaks, NO floating bokeh circles. SOFT COLOR GRADE: sun-bleached dust, faded red and cream, lifted soft blacks, low contrast — the same grade as every other era of this film. Shot on 35mm, fine period grain. Natural 180-degree shutter cadence — directional motion blur on fast moves, readable poses at the holds; no ghosting, no duplicated limbs, no strobing. ALL HANDHELD, organic, never chaotic, and every shot carries the train's constant sway. SFX only.

PACE: every shot lands its beat and cuts. Each cut arrives ON an action, never after it — no dead frames, no slowing down to fill time. The piece runs 18 seconds exactly and ends on the frame described in SHOT 10.

LAWS:
- SCREEN DIRECTION LOCK, the most important law: @train travels LEFT to RIGHT in every exterior shot, exactly as locked in @boards. On the roofs, the LOCOMOTIVE is always toward frame RIGHT and the rear of the train always toward frame LEFT, in every single roof shot without exception. @He_train moves toward frame RIGHT for the whole film — he is always working forward, toward the locomotive and toward her. The steam plume always shears back toward frame LEFT. This direction never flips in any shot, at any angle, for any reason.
- THE ROOF MAP, exactly as locked in @boards: counting back from the locomotive, @She_train is on the THIRD carriage and @He_train fights on the FOURTH, one carriage gap between them. Every roof BEHIND @He_train, all the way to the last carriage, is completely EMPTY for the whole film.
- CAST LOCK: exactly TWO named people exist in this film — @He_train and @She_train — and each appears exactly ONCE in any frame, never doubled. Besides them there are exactly THREE anonymous men in the entire film: ONE big guard on her roof in SHOTS 4 and 5, and TWO guards on his roof in SHOT 6. No one else is ever on the train, on the roofs, or on horseback after SHOT 2. No passengers are ever visible in any window.
- LIGHT CONTINUITY: ONE sun, high and hard, three-quarters behind the train, identical in every shot and across every hard cut — short hard-edged shadows, a pale hot sky, dust and heat haze in the far depth, the top of the steam plume catching the light. No cloud, no time passing, no change of key. The tunnel portal in SHOT 7 is never entered and its darkness never reaches the leads.
- BOTH OF THEM FIGHT, AND BOTH OF THEM TAKE SOMETHING: neither roof fight is one-sided. Her guard is a head taller and much heavier than she is; he lands hits, drives her backwards along the walkway and puts her on one knee at the roof's edge before she turns it. His two guards come at him together and get hands on him. Both leads win by leverage and timing rather than strength, and both carry it afterward — breathing hard, dust ground into a shoulder, torn canvas, a favoured arm. No blood, no wounds, no gore anywhere in the film.
- THE HATS STAY ON: both hats stay on both heads in every frame — through the board, the pull-up, both fights, the walk to the edge and the dive. The wind pulls at their duster coats and at her braid; the hats do not move. @She_train never touches or straightens her hat, not once, not even after the throw.
- IDENTITY UNDER MOTION: both faces stay photoreal, sharp and identity-locked in EVERY frame, and the same bone structure, proportions and features carry through fast motion, directional motion blur and slow motion alike. In the slow-motion passage — SHOT 9 — slow motion changes ONLY the speed of time: @She_train's face holds the exact geometry of her reference entering the ramp, through it and leaving it. Duster canvas, her braid and spray are the things that stretch; the face is not.
- NOBODY SHOOTS AND NOBODY DIES: not one shot is fired anywhere in this film. Both revolvers stay holstered in every frame — neither lead ever draws, and neither guard ever gets a gun clear. Both fights are hands only, both guards end unconscious and whole, and there is no blood, no wound and no gore anywhere in the film.
- THE LIVING TRAIN: the train is moving fast in every single frame. The world streams past behind every roof shot, the steam plume shears overhead, the whole train carries a constant fine sway, and every person on it stands and moves against that sway — knees loose, weight low, a hand out for balance. Nothing on this train is ever still.
- FACE RULE: a lead's visible face fills at least a THIRD of frame height, chest-up or closer, fully inside the frame; at distance a lead reads by silhouette, duster and hat; eyes never look into the lens.
- LIVING FACE: the eyes always WORK at a task, never pose; a blink lands ONLY on a change of thought; never a frozen glassy stare.
- LEVEL CAMERA: horizon level, zero roll, verticals upright — the train sways, the horizon does not.

SHOT 1 — 0.0–1.5s — THE CHASE — geography
84° diagonal field of view, airborne off the train's right flank, tracking level with the roofs. First frame: the whole @train ALREADY running left to right across open scrub, locomotive at frame RIGHT with its white plume shearing back, the six dark-red carriages trailing to frame LEFT. Below and alongside the last carriages, TWO riders gallop flat out through the dust of the ballast — @He_train on the nearer horse, hat low, duster streaming, closing on the rear platform. The camera drifts down toward him as he comes level with the last carriage.

SHOT 2 — 1.5–3.0s — HE BOARDS
Hard cut. 75° diagonal field of view, low and tight alongside the wheels, the ground ripping past underneath: @He_train stands in the stirrups, gets his timing on the sway, and THROWS himself at the rear platform's curved iron rail. Both hands catch it. His boots drag and skip along the ballast for a beat before he hauls his legs clear. The horse veers away out of frame LEFT, riderless. Cut as his boots leave the ground.

SHOT 3 — 3.0–4.5s — HE COMES UP
Hard cut. 84° diagonal field of view, low behind him on the rear platform: he gets a boot on the rail and a hand on the roof edge and does one real muscle-hard PULL-UP onto the roof of the last carriage — arms shaking, gut over the edge, a knee up, and he is on it. He straightens into the wind, hat pressed on, the empty iron walkways running away ahead of him toward frame RIGHT and the distant locomotive. Nothing behind him but empty roofs.

SHOT 4 — 4.5–6.5s — SHE IS DRIVEN BACK
Hard cut, three carriages forward. 84° diagonal field of view, handheld and level on her roof, the world streaming past: a BIG guard — a head taller and far heavier than @She_train — comes down the walkway and swings. She slips the first, blocks the second on her forearm and it drives her arm into her own face. He shoulders straight into her and she goes BACKWARDS along the walkway, boots skidding on the iron, fighting for balance against the sway. He grabs a fistful of her duster and swings her hard toward the roof's edge — she catches the walkway rail one-handed and stops herself with her boots half off the roof, the ground ripping past below her. She comes up onto one knee, breathing hard. He is already coming.

SHOT 5 — 6.5–8.5s — SHE TURNS IT
Hard cut. 75° diagonal field of view, low and close on the walkway: the big man reaches down to haul her up and OVERCOMMITS — she is already moving. She traps the reaching wrist, comes up under it, turns her hip into his weight and lets his own momentum carry him over. He goes off his feet and SLAMS flat onto the roof on his back, out cold, dead weight rocking with the sway. She rises off her knee, dust ground into one shoulder, one sleeve torn open, breathing hard. She does not look at him and she does NOT straighten her hat. She turns and keeps walking forward toward frame RIGHT.

SHOT 6 — 8.5–10.5s — HIS FIGHT
Hard cut. 84° diagonal field of view, handheld on his roof, the world streaming past: TWO guards come at @He_train along the walkway from frame RIGHT, together. The first gets both hands on him and grabs for the holstered revolver at his hip — @He_train's hand clamps over it and holds the gun down in the holster, a real grip contest, boots sliding on the iron, before he drives an elbow that drops the man. The second is already swinging; the punch lands across his jaw and rocks him back a full step — then he ducks the next one, takes the arm and puts the man down hard on the walkway. Both lie still. He straightens, spits dust, and is already moving forward again.

SHOT 7 — 10.5–12.0s — THE VIADUCT
Hard cut. 100° diagonal field of view, airborne wide off the train's right side over open water: the @train runs out onto a long stone-arched VIADUCT high above the sea, left to right, arches marching away below it, the water hard and bright a long way down, a rocky headland with a tunnel portal waiting at the frame-RIGHT end. Two small figures stand on the roofs, one behind the other, and the nearer one is closing. The world just fell away underneath them.

SHOT 8 — 12.0–14.0s — SHE WALKS TO THE EDGE
Hard cut. 75° diagonal field of view, level on her roof, the sea streaming past far below beyond the roof edge: @She_train walks unhurried and diagonally across the roof toward its seaward RIGHT edge and stops at the brink, boots square, wind dragging her torn duster out behind her. ACTING TASK — HER: task — go, and let him see her choose it; she looks back down the train at @He_train one time, level and unafraid, and something warm goes through it that she does not let reach her mouth. One slow blink. Cut as she turns back to the water.

SHOT 9 — 14.0–16.0s — THE DIVE — slow motion
Hard cut into smooth slow motion. 84° diagonal field of view, from the side and slightly above, the viaduct arches and the sea filling the depth: @She_train steps off the roof edge and commits — a clean confident dive, arms coming together, body straightening into a long line, hat still on, duster and braid streaming up behind her. The fall is LONG. The camera holds the arc and lets the train run on out of frame RIGHT above her. She enters the water in one small hard white splash, far below.

SHOT 10 — 16.0–18.0s — HE ARRIVES
Hard cut, real speed. 47° diagonal field of view (a 50mm-equivalent look), level on the roof: @He_train reaches the seaward edge of her carriage roof — the empty roof, the unconscious big man rocking with the sway behind him — and stops hard at the brink, one hand out for balance, breathing torn, a mark rising on his jaw. He looks DOWN and back at the water. ACTING TASK — HIM: task — find her; his eyes hunt the surface, the head turning a fraction behind them, and there is nothing to find. He does not shout and he does not reach. Final frame HELD: him at the roof's edge in profile against the bleached sky, the sea far below, one small ring of white water already sliding away behind the train. This exact frame is the last frame of the film, at 18.0s.

PHYSICS
Everything sits on a fast-moving train. The board in SHOT 2 has real closing speed and real impact — his boots drag and skip on the ballast before he clears them. The pull-up in SHOT 3 is genuinely hard: arms loaded, a slow grinding rise, a scrabbling knee. Both roof fights fight the sway — nobody has clean footing, every stance is wide and low, boots skid on iron, and both finishing throws use the attacker's own momentum rather than strength. The big man genuinely outweighs @She_train: when he shoulders into her she is moved, and when she stops herself at the roof edge it is a hard one-handed catch that jolts her whole body. The stopped draw is a real grip contest, two hands locked on one holster. Bodies that go down rock loosely with the train's motion. The wind on the roofs is constant and hard, dragging canvas out flat and pulling at hair, and it never lets up. Her dive is real ballistics: a genuine push-off, a clean arc, a long fall that reads its full height, and one small hard splash — not a plume, not an explosion of water. The train never slows, never stops and never changes speed.

SFX only: the pack of hooves and the ballast dust under them at the start, then the world of a train at speed — wheels hammering the rail joints in a steady rhythm, the deep rush of the slipstream, hard wind buffeting the lens on every roof shot, the steam plume roaring overhead, the carriages creaking and knocking with the sway, the far-off wail of the whistle. His grunt on the board, the iron rail ringing under his hands, boots dragging on gravel; the strained breath of the pull-up; the fights are wordless effort — cloth, breath, and two heavy body impacts on the roof. On the viaduct the sound goes suddenly hollow and drumming as the arches take the wheels, with the open sea underneath it. Through the slow-motion dive everything washes muffled and far away, leaving wind and one long fall — and one small hard splash. Then the wheels and the wind come back, and the piece ends inside them. No dialogue, no narration, no music of any kind. 35mm grain, sun-bleached classic-western realism.
