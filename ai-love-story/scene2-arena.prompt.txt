11 shots, 18 seconds total, 2.39:1, anamorphic. Hard cuts — fast-cut rhythm, every cut lands on an action. Slow motion ONLY inside SHOT 11. One continuous story: a young fighter is hunted, struck down and gets back up to take a war machine three times his size off its feet, while the woman who must not show she cares watches from the royal box.

SCENE ENGINE (production spine)
GOAL: he must put the machine on the ground; she must get through this without anyone seeing what she feels.
OBSTACLE: the machine outweighs him many times over and cannot be fought head-on; her every reaction is watched by a full amphitheatre.
TACTIC: he tries the obvious thing first and it fails — a blade to the leg does nothing and costs him a backhand across the arena. Only then does he stop fighting the front of the machine, take the crater rim, get ONTO its back and turn its own top-heavy mass against it. She holds her face still and lets only the smallest things through: a caught breath, a lean of a few centimeters.
REVERSAL: the reach that swatted him across the sand is the same reach that puts the machine on its back the moment he stops resisting it and rides it.
VALUE SHIFT: a boy sent out to die → he is not afraid → and it does not matter, the thing is untouchable → he is going to be killed → he got up → he wins, and does not finish it. Her verdict: a stranger on the sand → I cannot watch this → I am not going to look away.

REFERENCE DEFINITIONS

@He_arena: young fighter — face, hair, build and wardrobe match the reference exactly in every frame: fitted worn muscled bronze cuirass over a short pleated leather war-skirt with a frayed hem, leather baldric across the chest, bronze bracers on both forearms, sandals laced high up the shins; lean athletic build, sun-darkened arms and legs, short dark hair, short dark beard, no helmet. Reference.
@She_arena: young woman — face, hair and wardrobe match the reference exactly in every frame: floor-length ivory-white draped linen gown gathered under the bust, narrow gold cord belt, one bare shoulder with a gold brooch on the other, a wide engraved GOLD CUFF BRACELET on her right wrist, small gold drop earrings; long dark-brown hair dressed high in a braided coil with fine gold pins and a slim gold circlet, soft tendrils at the temples. Reference.
@robot: a colossal ADVANCED COMBAT AUTOMATON — a futuristic war machine, matching the reference exactly in every frame. Massively broad and top-heavy: thick angular plates of MATTE bone-white ceramic composite over a graphite-dark inner frame, clean panel gaps and recessed seams, scuffed, carbon-scored and field-repaired with mismatched panels. Never glossy, never chrome, never plastic-toy clean. Between the plates the inner mechanism shows: dense bundles of dark synthetic muscle fibre, braided hard-line conduit and machined actuator pistons; the joints are readable engineered assemblies — shoulder and hip rings, elbow and knee actuators, a segmented waist. Its head is a small armoured SENSOR HEAD set low between huge shoulders, crossed by one continuous horizontal VISOR BAND of dark glass with a thin cold CYAN-WHITE light behind it — no mouth, no nose, no eyes, no face of any kind. A colossal slab combat blade of dark matte alloy on a heavy haft in its RIGHT hand, one thin cyan line along the cutting edge. It does not belong to this place or this century, and it is not supposed to: ancient sun-bleached stone and a far-future machine in the same frame is the intended image of this scene. Reference.
@sketch: the height comparison drawing. It governs ONE thing and nothing else — the size ratio between @robot and @He_arena. Its line-art style, its white background and its poses never appear in the film. Reference.

TECHNICAL BLOCK
Cinematic photoreal, ultra filmic antique arena at midday. 21:9 — 35mm anamorphic film character: horizontal squeeze and compression, oval elliptical bokeh with stretched highlights, curved barrel edge distortion, subtle chromatic aberration toward the edges — all subtle and moderate; NO lens flares, NO streaks, NO floating bokeh circles. SOFT COLOR GRADE: sun-bleached warm sand and cream, lifted soft blacks, low contrast — the same grade as the other eras of this film carried into hard daylight. Shot on 35mm, fine period grain. Natural 180-degree shutter cadence — directional motion blur on fast moves, readable impact poses at the holds; no ghosting, no duplicated limbs, no strobing. ALL HANDHELD, organic, never chaotic. SFX only.

PACE: every shot lands its beat and cuts. Each cut arrives ON an action, never after it — no dead frames, no slowing down to fill time. The piece runs 18 seconds exactly and ends on the frame described in SHOT 9.

LAWS:
- THE SCALE, the single most important law: @robot stands THREE TIMES the height of @He_arena, exactly as locked in @sketch. In every frame that holds both of them, the top of @He_arena's head reaches only @robot's MID-THIGH — not his waist, not his chest. @robot's hand is as wide as @He_arena's torso; its forearm is thicker than @He_arena's body. This extreme ratio is intentional and is never softened toward realistic proportions in any shot, at any distance, in any framing. If @He_arena's head reaches @robot's waist or higher, the shot is wrong.
- CAST LOCK: exactly THREE named figures exist in this film — @He_arena, @She_arena and @robot — and each appears exactly ONCE in any frame, never doubled. SHOTS 2, 6 and 11 hold @She_arena alone in the loggia. Every other shot holds @He_arena and @robot together. Everyone else anywhere in frame is an anonymous crowd member in the far tiers.
- LOCATION LOCK: all nine shots take place in the SAME @arena: the same red-and-white banded wall, the same packed tiers and colonnade above, the same marble loggia with its awning and gilded throne, the same cratered sand underfoot.
- LIGHT CONTINUITY: ONE sun, high and almost vertical overhead, hard and unfiltered, identical in every shot and across every hard cut — short hard-edged shadows straight down, blazing sand, a bleached near-white sky, fine dust hanging and catching the light. The loggia sits under its awning in open shade with the same sun beyond it. No cloud, no time passing, no change of key anywhere in the film. @robot's own emissive light — the visor band, the chest core, the blade edge — is the ONLY cold light in the film: a hard cyan-white that never spills far, never lights the sand or the crowd, and never warms or shifts the sun. Warm ancient stone against one cold machine is the intended colour contrast of the scene.
- THE MACHINE FIGHTS BACK: @robot is an active opponent from the first second, not an obstacle standing still. It moves first, it attacks first, and it attacks repeatedly — it tracks him with the visor, swings the blade down at him twice, backhands him across the arena, and twists and claws at its own back while he climbs. It never simply waits. Its slowness is delay and commitment, never passivity: once a swing starts it cannot be recalled, and that is the only opening he ever gets.
- HE TAKES REAL DAMAGE: @He_arena is hit hard in SHOT 5 and it costs him. From SHOT 7 onward he carries it: heavier breathing, dust and grit driven into one shoulder and one side of his face, a torn baldric strap hanging, a favoured left arm, a stagger in the first stride back. No blood, no wound, no gore anywhere in the film — the damage reads through dust, torn leather, breath and the way he carries himself.
- NO FACE: @robot has no face and never acquires one. Through the strikes, the climb, the throw and the fall it shows only the armoured sensor head and its single horizontal visor band — no mouth, no nose, no eyes, no expression, ever. From behind it shows only the blank armoured nape and the dorsal spine. It never speaks and never emotes; everything it 'registers' reads as machine behaviour — the head tracking late, the visor light sweeping, the core brightening under load.
- IDENTITY UNDER MOTION: both leads' faces stay photoreal, sharp and identity-locked in EVERY frame, and the same bone structure, proportions and features carry through fast motion, directional motion blur and slow motion alike. In the slow-motion passage — SHOT 11 — slow motion changes ONLY the speed of time: @She_arena's face holds the exact geometry of her reference entering the ramp, through it and leaving it. Linen, tendrils and dust are the things that stretch; the face is not.
- SHE STAYS IN THE BOX: @She_arena never leaves the royal loggia and never stands on the sand. She is seen only from inside or just outside the box, the balustrade and the gilded armrest in frame with her, the arena always below her frame line.
- HE DOES NOT FINISH IT: the fight ends with @robot flat on its back and @He_arena standing over the exposed chest core holding the sword low. He never strikes the core. No blood anywhere in the film, on either of them — no wounds, no severed parts, no gore, no explosion, no fire.
- FACE RULE: a lead's visible face fills at least a THIRD of frame height, chest-up or closer, fully inside the frame; at distance a lead reads from behind or in profile; eyes never look into the lens.
- LIVING FACE: the eyes always WORK at a task, never pose; a blink lands ONLY on a change of thought, a few per shot; never a frozen glassy stare.
- LEVEL CAMERA: horizon level, zero roll, verticals upright.
- LIVING CROWD: the tiers are packed edge to edge in every frame that shows them, at the SAME density from the first frame to the last — standing, shouting, arms up, leaning over the rail, every figure different, all of them small in frame and never individually featured. They react to the fight, never to the lens.

SHOT 9 — 12.0–14.0s — THE THROW
Hard cut. 84° diagonal field of view, low and wide on the sand. @robot finally reaches back over its own shoulder and closes one enormous armoured hand around him — and @He_arena does not resist it: he rides the pull, plants both sandals against the shoulder ring and drives with his legs, turning the machine's own reaching momentum over the top. Top-heavy and slow to correct, the colossus goes over sideways and SLAMS flat onto the sand. PHYSICS
Everything obeys the mass difference. @He_arena moves fast, light and precise — real acceleration, a real planted takeoff off the crater rim, real grip and real strain climbing the actuator bundles and armour ledges. The overhead blade strikes carry enormous committed momentum: once a swing starts it cannot be recalled, it buries deep and has to be hauled back out, and that recovery time is the only opening he ever gets. The backhand in SHOT 5 transfers real mass into a much lighter body — he leaves the ground on the impact, travels far, tumbles with honest friction through the sand and hits the wall hard enough to crack plaster. @robot moves like a heavy machine, not like a man: every motion starts late and ends late, joints rotating on fixed axes with a moment of actuator slack before the limb follows, the whole top-heavy mass swaying a beat behind its own step. Servos load and settle audibly at each stop; nothing it does is fluid or organic. When it goes over it is dead metal weight — the sand craters under it, the impact jumps the ground, dust bursts outward in a low rolling wall and hangs. The throw is honest leverage, not strength: the machine's own reaching momentum carried over the top, and being top-heavy is exactly why it cannot recover. The slab blade bounces once and lies still. Cut conduit whips once and goes slack. Sparks off the knee actuator in SHOT 4 spray and die on the sand and do not set anything alight. Linen, tendrils and the awning move with the hot air. Every figure solid, every contact shadow correct on the sand.

SFX only: the amphitheatre ROARS — a packed antique crowd, shouting, stamping on stone, a rolling wall of voices. Under it: @He_arena's sandals hammering the sand, his breath tearing, the creak of conduit taking his weight, and the machine — a deep sub-bass reactor hum under everything, heavy servo whine loading and settling at each move, the hard clack of armour plate against plate, a sharp pneumatic hiss from the vents at each step. The blade strikes land as deep sand impacts with a hard metallic ring; the xiphos skating off armour rings thin and useless; the backhand lands as one heavy body impact and a crack of plaster. The crowd's single shocked "OOOH" on the hit. The THROW lands as one enormous metal impact — a deep ground thud, armour ringing, a burst of dust, the blade clattering — then pressure venting, servos winding down, and the reactor hum sinking away to nothing. Then the crowd EXPLODES. Through the slow-motion last shot everything dips muffled and far away, leaving one close breath and the hot wind moving the awning — and the piece ends inside it. No dialogue, no narration, no music of any kind. 35mm grain, sun-bleached historical-epic realism.
